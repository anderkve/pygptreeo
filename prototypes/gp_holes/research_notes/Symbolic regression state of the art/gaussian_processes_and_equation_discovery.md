## 1. For each paper: what is the model, what is searched over or selected, and by what criterion?

### Takeaway
The listed papers fall into four groups: (a) a symbolic part that is linear in a fixed library plus one additive GP, with exact evidence and sparsity priors (Zhu22, with KOH as its ancestor); (b) search or continuous selection over GP kernel structure by evidence or BIC (CKS, ABCD, AGP, OAK, arXiv 2509.25946); (c) a GP or kernel fit used as a smoother that supplies function values and derivatives to a separate sparse regression (KBASS, GPSINDy, GP-SINDy), or with the operator's parameters as kernel hyperparameters (Raissi17); (d) neural or other approximators for the unknown part followed by symbolic regression (UDE, AI-Lorenz), plus GP-KAN, which has no symbolic step at all.
None of them searches over nonlinear symbolic structure with a GP inside the expression and the evidence as score.

### Cited Findings

#### Zhu et al. 2022 (GP-RVM)
- Model: `y = f(x,θ) + δ(x,ψ) + ε` with `f(x,θ) = Φθ` (Φ an N×M design matrix of candidate basis functions), `δ ~ GP(m(x|ψ), k(x,x'|ψ))`, `ε` i.i.d. `N(0,σ²)`. — [Zhu22, Sec. 2, Eqs. (1)–(3)](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- Sparsity prior: `p(θ|α) = N(0, A⁻¹)`, `A = diag(α)`; `α_i → ∞` makes `p(θ_i|α_i)` a delta at zero and removes term i. — [Zhu22, Sec. 3, Eq. (8)](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- Both θ and δ are integrated out analytically: `p(y|α,ψ,σ²) = N(y | m, C)` with `C = σ²I + K + Φ A⁻¹ Φᵀ`; log evidence `L = −(N/2) log 2π − ½ log det C − ½ (y−m)ᵀ C⁻¹ (y−m)`. — [Zhu22, Sec. 3, Eqs. (11), (13), (14)](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- Posterior of the coefficients: `θ | y ~ N(μ, Σ)`, `Σ = [Φᵀ(σ²I+K)⁻¹Φ + A]⁻¹`, `μ = Σ Φᵀ (σ²I+K)⁻¹ (y−m)`. — [Zhu22, Eqs. (15)–(17)](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- Criterion: type-II maximum likelihood over (α, ψ, σ²) with a uniform hyperprior. — [Zhu22, Sec. 3, text between Eqs. (12) and (13)](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- The authors state that the global maximum of Eq. (11) may sit at θ = 0 with the GP "taking over the role of the system model", which "defeats the initial purpose"; they therefore use EM "finding the maximum of the likelihood function where the system model f dominates". — [Zhu22, end of Sec. 3](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- EM: θ is the latent variable; `α_i ← 1/(Σ_ii + μ_i²)`; ψ and σ² are updated by maximising `Q2` (Eq. 23). The iteration starts from ψ = 0 (no GP) and σ² = 1% of the output variance, and "uses an EM method to find the physically relevant maximum (regardless of whether it is global or not) where the system model dominates by deliberately controlling the initialisation point". — [Zhu22, Sec. 4, Eqs. (18)–(24), and Sec. 6](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- Analytic add/delete rule for one term given the rest: with `C₋ᵢ = C − α_i⁻¹ φ_i φ_iᵀ`, `q_i = φ_iᵀ C₋ᵢ⁻¹ (y−m)`, `s_i = φ_iᵀ C₋ᵢ⁻¹ φ_i`: `α_i = s_i²/(q_i² − s_i)` if `q_i² > s_i`, else `α_i = ∞` (term removed). `q_i, s_i` follow from `Q_i = φ_iᵀ C⁻¹ (y−m)`, `S_i = φ_iᵀ C⁻¹ φ_i` by `q_i = α_i Q_i/(α_i − S_i)`, `s_i = α_i S_i/(α_i − S_i)`. — [Zhu22, Sec. 5, Eqs. (25)–(36)](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- Procedure: start with one basis function, cycle over candidates applying re-estimate / add / delete, update (ψ, σ²) by maximising Eq. (23), stop at relative evidence change below 1e-6. — [Zhu22, Sec. 6](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- Experiments use a zero-mean GP with a squared-exponential kernel. — [Zhu22, Sec. 7, Eq. (37)](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- Toy: truth `5x³ + x² + 2 sin x`, 100 points uniform in [−1, 1], noise s.d. 10% of the output, candidate library `[x³, x⁵]` (so `x²` and `sin x` are missing and `x⁵` is wrong). Over 100 data sets GP-RVM excludes `x⁵` in all of them and gives `a` close to 5; plain RVM is biased. NMSE of the mean prediction: 3.4e-4 (RVM) against 8.8e-5 (GP-RVM). About 20 EM iterations. — [Zhu22, Sec. 7.1, Figs. 1–4](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- Duffing oscillator `m ÿ + c ẏ + (a + b y²) y = u`: 10 s at 50 Hz (500 samples), noise 1% of the response s.d., 10 data sets per scenario, GP input = displacement only. With the stiffness term left out of the library: average bias of mass and damping 1.60% and 3.11% (RVM) against 0.03% and 0.21% (GP-RVM); NMSE 4.4e-4 against 9.4e-6. With wrong stiffness forms in the library: RVM keeps them, GP-RVM removes them in all data sets. With the true library both methods agree ("the flexibility of the GP does not reduce the explanatory power of the system model terms"). — [Zhu22, Sec. 7.2, Tables 2–4](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- Stated limit: if the GP is given all system states as inputs "the GP model will become more flexible and more likely to take over the role of the system model. In this case, balancing the explanatory power between the system model and the GP model can be challenging and it would form a potential topic for future work." — [Zhu22, end of Sec. 7.2](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- Silverbox (measured data): 40 data sets of 1000 samples; both methods select the linear terms; `y³` is selected in about 30% of data sets by both; RVM selects the wrong terms (`y²`, `cos y`) more often than GP-RVM. — [Zhu22, Sec. 7.3, Fig. 8](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- Stated assumptions: the system model is linear in the basis functions (Eq. 2), parameters are independent, all inputs are known. — [Zhu22, Sec. 8](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)

#### Kennedy & O'Hagan 2001 (summary of the discrepancy construction)
- Model: `z_i = ζ(x_i) + e_i = ρ η(x_i, θ) + δ(x_i) + e_i`, with η the computer code, θ the calibration parameters, ρ an unknown regression parameter, δ a "model inadequacy function that is independent of the code output", `e_i ~ N(0, λ)`. — [KOH, Sec. 4.2, Eqs. (5)–(6), p. 11–12 of this copy](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/statistics/comp_exp/jour.club/kennedy01.pdf)
- Both η and δ get GP priors with linear-model means `m₁ = h₁ᵀβ₁`, `m₂ = h₂ᵀβ₂` and a flat prior `p(β₁, β₂) ∝ 1`. — [KOH, Sec. 4.2, Eq. (7)](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/statistics/comp_exp/jour.club/kennedy01.pdf)
- On the meaning of the fitted parameters: the "true" θ is a best-fitting θ, and "it is dangerous to interpret the estimates of θ obtained by calibration as estimates of the true physical values of those parameters". — [KOH, Sec. 4.3, p. 13](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/statistics/comp_exp/jour.club/kennedy01.pdf)
- Inference: β is integrated out analytically ("the exponent of (9) is quadratic in β"); the GP hyperparameters are not integrated but estimated in two stages (code-output hyperparameters from the code runs, then ρ, λ and the discrepancy hyperparameters from the observations) and then held fixed; the authors say this "means that we do not account fully for all these sources" of uncertainty. — [KOH, Secs. 4.4–4.5, Eq. (9), p. 14](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/statistics/comp_exp/jour.club/kennedy01.pdf)

#### Duvenaud et al. 2013 (CKS) and Lloyd et al. 2014 (ABCD)
- Model: GP regression whose kernel is an algebraic expression in base kernels combined with + and ×. CKS base kernels: SE, Per, Lin, RQ, each one-dimensional, applied to single input dimensions. — [CKS, Secs. 2–3 and Appendix](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.4922)
- ABCD base kernels: WN, C, Lin, SE, Per, plus changepoint `CP(k₁,k₂) = σ(x)k₁σ(x') + (1−σ(x))k₂(1−σ(x'))` and changewindow operators. — [ABCD, Sec. 2, Eqs. (2.1)–(2.3), App. A](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1402.4304)
- What the kernels encode: Lin = Bayesian linear regression, Lin×Lin×… = polynomial regression, `Σ SE_d` = generalised additive model, `Π SE_d` = ARD, `Lin + SE` = linear trend with local deviations, `Lin × SE` = linearly growing amplitude. — [CKS, Sec. 2, "Example expressions"](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.4922)
- Criterion: marginal likelihood at optimised kernel parameters, penalised by BIC: `BIC(M) = −2 log p(D|M) + |M| log n`, `|M|` the number of kernel parameters. — [ABCD, Sec. 3, Eq. (3.1)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1402.4304); [CKS, Sec. 3, "Scoring kernel families"](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.4922)
- Search details are in question 4.

#### Duvenaud, Nickisch, Rasmussen 2011 (Additive GPs)
- Kernel: n-th order additive kernel `k_addn(x,x') = σ_n² Σ_{i1<…<in} Π_d k_{id}(x_{id}, x'_{id})`; the full kernel is the sum over all orders 1…D; with SE base kernels the D-th order term is the SE-ARD kernel. — [AGP, Sec. 3, Eqs. (1)–(5)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1112.4394)
- What is selected: the D "order variance" hyperparameters `σ_1²…σ_D²` and the base-kernel length-scales, by maximising the marginal likelihood (L-BFGS, 500 iterations, five random restarts). No discrete search. — [AGP, Secs. 3.1 and 5.2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1112.4394)
- Computation: the sum over all terms of order n is the n-th elementary symmetric polynomial of the base-kernel values, evaluated by the Newton–Girard recursion in O(D²); Gram matrix cost O(N²DR) for maximum order R. — [AGP, Secs. 3.3–3.4, Eqs. (6)–(7)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1112.4394)
- Reported order variances (Table 1) differ widely by data set, e.g. pumadyn-8nh 99.5% at order 8, concrete 70.6% at order 1, housing 80.6% at order 3. — [AGP, Table 1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1112.4394)

#### Lu, Boukouvalas, Hensman 2022 (OAK)
- Same additive kernel, but each base kernel is replaced by a constrained kernel so that `∫ f_i(x_i) p_i(x_i) dx_i = 0`: `k̃_i(x,x') = k_i(x,x') − E[S_i f_i(x)] E[S_i²]⁻¹ E[S_i f_i(x')]`, with `E[S_i f_i(·)] = ∫ p_i(x) k_i(x,·) dx`, `E[S_i²] = ∫∫ p_i p_i k_i`; interaction terms use products of constrained kernels; a constant kernel carries the mean. — [OAK, Sec. 3.1, Eqs. (6)–(9)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.09861)
- Closed form for an SE base kernel and Gaussian input density `N(μ, δ²)`: `k̃(x,x') = exp(−(x−x')²/2l²) − [l√(l²+2δ²)/(l²+δ²)] exp(−((x−μ)² + (x'−μ)²)/(2(l²+δ²)))`. The text says closed forms also exist for mixture-of-Gaussian, uniform, categorical and empirical input measures, and that the Matérn kernel also gives analytic expressions. — [OAK, Sec. 3.2, Eq. (10)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.09861)
- Empirical-measure version for any kernel: `E[S f(·)] ≈ Σ_i w_i k(x, x_i)`, `E[S²] ≈ Σ_i Σ_j w_i w_j k(x_i, x_j)`. — [OAK, App. E, Eq. (32)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.09861)
- What is selected: hyperparameters by maximum likelihood with a Gamma prior (MAP) on the variance hyperparameters; then terms are ranked by the Sobol index of their posterior mean and truncated below a normalised index of 0.01 (default). — [OAK, Sec. 5 and App. H](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.09861)

#### Raissi, Perdikaris, Karniadakis 2017
- Model: `f(x) = L_x^φ u(x)` with L a linear operator of given form and unknown parameters φ; `u ~ GP(0, k_uu(θ))` implies `f ~ GP(0, k_ff)` with `k_ff = L_x^φ L_{x'}^φ k_uu`, `k_uf = L_{x'}^φ k_uu`. — [Raissi17, Secs. 1 and 2.1, Eqs. (1)–(2)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1701.02440)
- Criterion: negative log marginal likelihood of the joint data `[y_u; y_f] ~ N(0, K)` with K the 2×2 block matrix of `k_uu + σ_nu² I`, `k_uf`, `k_fu`, `k_ff + σ_nf² I`, minimised by L-BFGS over (φ, θ, σ_nu², σ_nf²). "The parameters φ of the operator are turned into hyper-parameters of the kernels." — [Raissi17, Sec. 2.2, Eqs. (3)–(4)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1701.02440)
- Nothing is searched: the operator's form is given; only its parameters are fitted. "In its current form … the proposed framework cannot deal with non-linear equations." — [Raissi17, Sec. 4](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1701.02440)
- Special case named in the introduction: `κ = φ·δ` gives recursive co-kriging `f(x) = φ u(x) + v(x)`, where "v(x) is a latent function and is included to capture unexplained factors". — [Raissi17, Sec. 1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1701.02440)

#### Rackauckas et al. 2020 (UDE)
- Model: a differential equation "defined in full or part by a universal approximator" `U_θ` (neural network, Fourier or Chebyshev expansion): `N[u(t), u(α(t)), W(t), U_θ(u, β(t))] = 0`. Lotka–Volterra example: `ẋ = αx + U₁(θ,x,y)`, `ẏ = −θ₁ y + U₂(θ,x,y)`. — [UDE, Sec. 1, Eq. (1) and Sec. 2.3.1, Eqs. (2)–(3)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2001.04385)
- Criterion for the fit: a loss on the solved trajectory, e.g. `C(θ) = Σ_i ||u_θ(t_i) − d_i||`, minimised by ADAM/BFGS through adjoint sensitivities. — [UDE, Sec. 2.1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2001.04385)
- Symbolic step: sparse regression (SINDy variants; SR3 with a sweep of thresholds and Pareto selection on the L1 norm of coefficients against the L2 error) applied to the output of the trained approximator, in place of the derivative data. — [UDE, Supplement Sec. 11 and 11.1.1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2001.04385)
- Numbers: 31 points, Δt = 0.1, noise 5% of the mean; network with two hidden layers of 5 neurons; recovered β ≈ 0.9239, γ ≈ 0.8145 (truth 0.9, 0.8). Over 500 runs at noise 0.1–5% the recovery rate was (50.4 ± 25.7)% of 498 error-free runs. — [UDE, Supplement Sec. 11.1.1, Figs. 8–9](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2001.04385)

#### AI-Lorenz (De Florio, Kevrekidis, Karniadakis 2023)
- Model: a small random-projection network (X-TFC) fits the time series and its time derivative ("black-box"), or the missing term of a partly known ODE system ("gray-box", e.g. `ẏ = f(x,y,z) − y` in the Lorenz system); the learned right-hand sides are passed to PySR. — [AI-Lorenz, Sec. 2, Fig. 1, and Sec. 2.2, Eq. (16)](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.14237)
- Criterion: PySR's own fitness (MSE-type) with genetic search; no evidence. — [AI-Lorenz, Sec. 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.14237)
- GP comparison: scikit-learn GP regression of the trajectories, then numerical differentiation, then PySR. MAE of the learned `ẋ`: GPR 2.25 (200 points), 9.94e-2 (1000), 2.49e-2 (2000); X-TFC 1.73e-1, 1.77e-3, 1.14e-5. GPR time 6.6 s to 8 min; X-TFC about 1–2 s. With GPR input PySR finds exact equations only from 1000 points on. — [AI-Lorenz, Sec. 3.1.3, Table 6, Fig. 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.14237)
- Noise: 1000 points with Gaussian noise of s.d. 0.1, 0.5, 1, 2; exact equations for 0.1 and 0.5, a term missing for 1 and 2; the learned right-hand sides are smoothed with a Savitzky–Golay filter first. — [AI-Lorenz, Sec. 3.1.4, Figs. 9–10](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.14237)

#### "Automated model discovery via multi-modal & multi-step pipeline" (arXiv 2509.25946)
- Model class: GP kernel compositions over base kernels LIN, PER, SE, C, WN with +, × and replacement, i.e. the ABCD grammar. — [2509.25946, App. A.1, Eqs. (4)–(6)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25946)
- Search: a vision-language model ("AnalyzerVLM") proposes candidate kernels after multi-step analysis with generated code and plots; parameters are fitted by marginal likelihood with random restarts and inheritance; a second model ("EvaluatorVLM") scores plots. — [2509.25946, Sec. 3.1, Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25946)
- Criterion: `VIC(M,D) = α·EvaluatorVLM(M, θ*, D) − BIC`, with `BIC = −2 log p(D|M,θ*) + |M| log|D|`. — [2509.25946, Sec. 3.3, Eqs. (2)–(3)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25946)
- Settings: 5 rounds, 10 random restarts, L-BFGS-B, top-3 sampling from the pool, α = 50, on GPy and GPy-ABCD. — [2509.25946, App. A.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25946)
- Symbolic regression enters in two ways. Sec. 4.3 reuses the pipeline for functions instead of kernels, with the BIC term replaced by "normalized mean squared error combined with a complexity penalty" (20 rounds, 5 restarts, scipy curve fit, α = 0.05). App. A.10 describes a hybrid: a simple symbolic regressor proposes function compositions, the top three are turned into "corresponding kernel structure with its initial parameters", then two rounds of kernel discovery follow. — [2509.25946, Sec. 4.3, App. A.9–A.10, Table A7](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25946)
- Stated limitation: one-dimensional data sets only. — [2509.25946, "Limitations"](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25946)

#### GP-KAN (Chen 2024)
- Model: a Kolmogorov–Arnold network in which each edge function is a one-dimensional GP conditioned on trainable inducing points `(h, z)`; the only multi-input operation is addition. — [GP-KAN, Sec. 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18397)
- Key construction: for a Gaussian input `p(x) = N(μ_x, σ_x²)` the neuron output is the inner product `ỹ = ∫ f̃(x) p(x) dx`, which is Gaussian with closed-form mean `√(2π) s² l q_xhᵀ K_hh⁻¹ h` and variance `s² l/√(l² + 2σ_x²) − 2π s⁴ l² q_xh K_hh⁻¹ q_hx` for the SE kernel. — [GP-KAN, Sec. 3.1, Eqs. (6)–(11)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18397)
- Criterion: log-likelihood of the outputs, by gradient descent on inducing points, noise and kernel parameters. Nothing is searched and no formula is produced. — [GP-KAN, Abstract and Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18397)
- Toy: `y = exp(sin(πx₁) + x₂²)` with a 2-1 layout on inputs in [−0.5, 0.5]; the three neurons are reported to take the shapes of `sin(πx₁)`, `x₂²` and `eˣ` (the last "less stable with some oscillatory features"). Main experiment is MNIST (98.5% with 80 thousand parameters). — [GP-KAN, Sec. 3.2, Fig. 2, and Sec. 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18397)

#### Wycoff, Binois, Wild 2021
- Target: the active-subspace matrix `C = ∫ (∇f)(∇f)ᵀ dμ`; its leading eigenvectors span the directions along which f varies. — [Wycoff21, Sec. 2.1, Eqs. (1)–(2)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.11572)
- Closed form given a fitted GP (Theorem 2): `C_ij⁽ⁿ⁾ = E_ij − tr(K_n⁻¹ W_ij) + y_nᵀ K_n⁻¹ W_ij K_n⁻¹ y_n`, with `W_ij = ∫ κ_i(x)ᵀ κ_j(x) dμ`, `E_ij = ∫ ∂²k(x,x)/∂x_i∂x_j dμ`, `κ_i(x) = Cov[Y(X_n), ∂Y(x)/∂x_i]`, `K_n` including the noise variance τ². — [Wycoff21, Sec. 3.1, Theorem 2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.11572)
- `E_ij` and `W_ij` are in closed form for tensor-product Gaussian and Matérn (ν = 3/2, 5/2) kernels. — [Wycoff21, Sec. 3.1 after Theorem 2, App. A](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.11572)
- What is selected: the subspace dimension is read from a gap in the eigenvalues; the paper's own aim is sequential design (acquisition functions based on the variance of the update of C). — [Wycoff21, Secs. 2.1 and 3.4](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.11572)

#### Long et al. 2023 (KBASS)
- Model: PDE `u_t − Σ_j w_j P_j[u] = 0` with operators from a dictionary; u is a kernel interpolant from values U on a mesh, `u(z) = κ(z, M) K_MM⁻¹ vec(U)`; derivatives are taken on the kernel, e.g. `∂_{x1x2} u(z) = ∂_{x1x2} κ(z, M) K_MM⁻¹ vec(U)`. — [KBASS, Sec. 3, Eqs. (3)–(5)](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05387v2)
- Selection: spike-and-slab prior `p(s_j) = Bern(ρ₀)`, `p(w_j|s_j) = s_j N(0, σ₀²) + (1−s_j) δ(w_j)`; a "virtual" likelihood `N(h | Φw, τI)` ties `u_t` on the mesh to the operator values; data likelihood `N(y_tr | u_tr, vI)`. Operators are kept when the posterior `q(s_j = 1)` exceeds a level (0.5 in the text), with no threshold on weights. — [KBASS, Secs. 2–3, Eqs. (2), (6), (7)](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05387v2)
- Inference: EP for `q(s, w)` (E step), then ADAM on `L = −½ vec(U)ᵀ K_MM⁻¹ vec(U) − (1/2v)||y − f||² − (1/2τ) E_q||h − Φ̂ŵ||²` over U and the kernel parameters (M step). The function estimate is a point estimate with an RKHS-norm penalty; it is not integrated out. — [KBASS, Sec. 4, Eqs. (11)–(13), Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05387v2)
- Cost: Kronecker structure on the mesh gives `O(Σ_j d_j³ + (N + Σ_j d_j) d)`. — [KBASS, Sec. 4, "Algorithm Complexity"](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05387v2)
- Data regimes: Van der Pol with 10 or 25 points and 0%, 1%, 20% noise, 24 candidate terms; Lorenz 96 with 12 or 50 points, 84 candidates. — [KBASS, Sec. 6, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05387v2)

#### GP smoothing before sparse regression: GPSINDy and GP-SINDy
- GPSINDy: (i) a GP in time with SE kernel smooths the states, `X_GP = K(t*, t)[K(t,t) + θ_n² I]⁻¹ X`; (ii) a second GP, whose kernel is a function of the smoothed state (and control), smooths the finite-difference derivatives; (iii) LASSO on a library (polynomials to third order, sin, cos, products) with λ = 0.1, solved by ADMM. Kernels are chosen by marginal likelihood. — [GPSINDy, Sec. III, Eqs. (8)–(19)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.11076v3)
- GPSINDy does not differentiate the GP: "we should benchmark our method against directly taking the derivative of Gaussian Processes" is listed as future work. — [GPSINDy, Sec. V](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.11076v3)
- GPSINDy regimes: Lotka–Volterra and unicycle with noise s.d. 0.05 to 0.25, 40 seeds; JetRacer hardware at 5–50 Hz with added noise 0 to 0.1; for the unicycle "each method failed to identify the true coefficients when 3rd order terms were included". — [GPSINDy, Secs. IV-A to IV-C, Fig. 1, Table II](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.11076v3)
- GP-SINDy (Meng & Qiu): one GP per state over the space-time inputs; derivatives are taken analytically from the kernel (posterior mean and posterior variance of `∂f`), and the posterior covariance Σ_i of the time derivative weights the regression: `min (U̇_i − ΦC_i)ᵀ W_i (U̇_i − ΦC_i)`, `Ĉ_i = (ΦᵀW_iΦ)⁻¹ΦᵀW_i U̇_i`, best linear unbiased for `W_i = Σ_i⁻¹`; sparsity by sequential thresholding (STWLS). — [GP-SINDy, Secs. 3.1.2, 3.2, Eqs. (5)–(9), Theorem 1, Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.11825)
- GP-SINDy regimes: noise ratios 0.05 to 0.3, grids such as 41×65 down to 41×17; with sparse data and noise 0.2 the plain version "fails when the noise level is large". — [GP-SINDy, Sec. 4, Tables 2–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.11825)

#### Snelson, Rasmussen, Ghahramani 2003 (Warped GPs)
- Model: latent `z_n = f(t_n; Ψ)` with f monotonic and onto the real line, z a GP. Negative log likelihood with the Jacobian term: `L = ½ log det C_N + ½ f(t_N)ᵀ C_N⁻¹ f(t_N) − Σ_n log ∂f(t)/∂t|_{t_n} + (N/2) log 2π`. — [WGP, Sec. 3, Eq. (6)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2003/file/6b5754d737784b51ec5075c0dc437bf0-Paper.pdf)
- Warp family: `f(t; Ψ) = t + Σ_i a_i tanh(b_i (t + c_i))`, `a_i, b_i ≥ 0`; Θ and Ψ are trained together by conjugate gradients on Eq. (6). — [WGP, Secs. 3.1 and 4, Eqs. (12)–(13)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2003/file/6b5754d737784b51ec5075c0dc437bf0-Paper.pdf)
- Prediction: the median is the inverse warp of the latent mean; the mean needs a one-dimensional Gauss–Hermite integral. — [WGP, Sec. 3.2](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2003/file/6b5754d737784b51ec5075c0dc437bf0-Paper.pdf)

## 2. Does any of them have a model "symbolic part plus or times a GP for what is not known"? Is the GP marginalised exactly, is the symbolic part searched or given, is the GP meant to stay?

### Takeaway
Yes for "plus", in a restricted form: Zhu et al. 2022 integrate out exactly both the coefficients of a linear symbolic library and an additive GP discrepancy, and select library terms with a sparsity prior; the GP stays in the answer. No paper read here has a GP multiplied by a symbolic factor, a GP of symbolic inputs, or a search over nonlinear symbolic structure scored by the evidence with the GP integrated out. Two further works found by search come close in parts: EGP (symbolic bases found by genetic programming inside a GP mean, plug-in maximum likelihood) and SJEPA (writes down "symbolic law + marginalised GP + structure prior" but runs only a neural version).

## 3. Which of them detect structure of an unknown function from a fitted GP, how, and with how many points and how much noise?

### Takeaway
Additivity and interactions: AGP (order variances) and OAK (orthogonal components, analytic Sobol indices of the posterior mean), on hundreds to thousands of real data points. Low-dimensional linear subspaces: Wycoff21, in closed form from the GP posterior, tested with 15 points per input dimension and almost no noise. Differential equations: Raissi17 (parameters of a given linear operator, from 7 to 40 points), KBASS and GP-SINDy (terms from a dictionary, from 10 to a few thousand points, noise up to 20–30%). None of the papers read detects symmetries (scaling, translation) or power-law and exponential factors from a fitted GP.

### Inferences
- Our symmetry rule (linear relations between the hole's posterior mean and its derivatives at the data points, in the coordinates u, log u, u², 1/u) has no counterpart among the papers read. ## 4. Compositional kernel search: organisation of the greedy search, use of BIC, cost and failure modes

### Takeaway
The search is plain greedy hill-climbing: expand only the best kernel of the current level with every operator at every sub-expression, fit each candidate from inherited parameters with random restarts for the new ones, score by BIC, repeat to depth 10. The papers give no run times and no candidate counts; the failure modes they name are local optima in the parameter fit (harmonics), back-off to simpler structures as noise grows, overfitting without the BIC penalty, and BIC favouring many-component kernels.

### Cited Findings
- Start: CKS begins "by proposing all base kernel families applied to all input dimensions"; ABCD starts from the noise kernel WN. — [CKS, Sec. 3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.4922); [ABCD, App. B.1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1402.4304)
- Operators (CKS): (1) any sub-expression `S → S + B`; (2) `S → S × B`; (3) any base kernel `B → B'`, with B any base kernel family (on any input dimension). "At each stage, we choose the highest scoring kernel and expand it by applying all possible operators." — [CKS, Sec. 3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.4922)
- Extra operators (ABCD): `S → CP(S,S)`, `S → CW(S,S)`, `S → CW(S,C)`, `S → CW(C,S)`; and, "based on experience with typical paths followed by the search algorithm", `S → S × (B + C)`, `S → B`, `S + S' → S`, `S × S' → S`, of which the paper says "Their introduction is currently not rigorously justified." — [ABCD, App. B.2, Eqs. (B.1)–(B.11)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1402.4304)
- Motivation of the operators: (1) corresponds to finding structure in the residuals and adding it; (2) to global structure that holds only locally; both add input dimensions one at a time, like forward selection. — [CKS, Sec. 3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.4922)
- Parameter fits: "all of the parameters which were part of the previous kernel are initialized to their previous values. All parameters are then optimized using conjugate gradients, randomly restarting the newly introduced parameters." ABCD: 10 random restarts and depth 10 in all experiments. — [CKS, Sec. 3, "Scoring kernel families"](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.4922); [ABCD, App. B.1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1402.4304)
- BIC: the integral over kernel parameters is "intractable" and is approximated "with the Bayesian information criterion after first optimizing to find the maximum-likelihood kernel parameters"; `BIC(M) = −2 log p(D|M) + |M| log n` with `|M|` the number of kernel parameters. — [CKS, Sec. 3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.4922); [ABCD, Sec. 3, Eq. (3.1)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1402.4304)
- Without the penalty: "We experimented with using unpenalised marginal likelihood as the search criterion but observed overfitting, as is to be expected." — [ABCD, Sec. 8, footnote 2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1402.4304)
- Failure mode, local optima: "optimizing over parameters is not a convex optimization problem, and the space can have many local optima. For example, in data with periodic structure, integer multiples of the true period (i.e. harmonics) are often local optima." The inheritance of parameters "is not guaranteed to find the global optimum". — [CKS, Sec. 3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.4922)
- Failure mode, noise: Table 1 (300 points). At SNR 10 all structures are found in all but one test: `SE + RQ` comes back as `SE`, and `(SE₁+SE₂)×(SE₃+SE₄)` comes back with extra `Lin` factors, explained by "functions sampled from SE kernels with long length scales occasionally have near-linear trends". At SNR 1 and 0.1 "our method generally backs off to simpler structures". — [CKS, Sec. 6, Table 1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.4922)
- Failure mode, model class: on the solar data "none of the models in our search space are capable of parsimoniously representing the lack of variation from 1645 to 1715"; the result still has the periodic structure and a fast-growing predictive variance. — [CKS, Sec. 5](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.4922)
- Failure mode, BIC and nesting: BIC counts parameters, which "can result in ABCD favoring kernel expressions with nested products of sums, producing descriptions involving many additive components". Remedy tried: distribute all products over sums during the search, so that such models are penalised more ("ABCD-interpretability"); it predicts slightly worse than the unrestricted search. — [ABCD, Sec. 8, "Interpretability versus accuracy", Fig. 12](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1402.4304)
- Design fixes for interpretability: the RQ kernel was removed because one component captured both long-term trend and short-term variation; constants were subtracted from Lin and Per because otherwise products expand into many qualitatively different terms and "the constant functions can result in anti-correlation between components in the posterior, resulting in inflated credible intervals for each component". — [ABCD, Sec. 6, Figs. 10–11](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1402.4304)
- Simplification before description: distribute products over sums; `SE × SE = SE`; multiplying WN by a stationary kernel gives WN; and similar rules. — [ABCD, Sec. 4](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1402.4304)
- Decomposition of the result: `f₁ | f ~ N(μ₁ + K₁(K₁+K₂)⁻¹(f − μ₁ − μ₂), K₁ − K₁(K₁+K₂)⁻¹K₁)` gives the posterior of each additive component. — [CKS, Appendix, "Posterior decomposition"](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.4922)
- Performance claims: on 5 regression data sets (10-fold cross-validation, depth 10, SE and RQ only) the search beats the next-best method in each test, "although not substantially". — [CKS, Sec. 7.2, Table 2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.4922)
- Cost: neither paper reports wall-clock time or the number of candidates evaluated (no such statement found in either text). A follow-up paper states the limit: the Automatic Statistician "does not scale due to its O(N³) running time for the model selection", and proposes bounds on the marginal likelihood to prune candidates; it also reports a failure mode of using only the variational lower bound for selection. — [Kim & Teh 2018, Abstract and Sec. 4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.02524)
- The later LLM-driven variant keeps a pool, samples the top 3 per round as parents, runs 5 rounds with 10 restarts, and argues that the marginal likelihood "may struggle to generalize to unseen regions". — [2509.25946, Sec. 3.3 and App. A.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25946)

### Inferences
- Candidates per step (my count, not in the papers): a kernel with s sub-expressions and b base kernels, with B base families and D input dimensions, gives about `2·s·B·D` candidates from operators (1) and (2) and `b·(B·D − 1)` from (3). For a one-dimensional series with ABCD's five families and the extra operators this is some tens of candidates per sub-expression, each fitted with 10 restarts, for 10 levels.
- CKS keeps a beam of width one and never revisits a rejected branch; ABCD's removal operators (B.9–B.11) are the only way back. Our search keeps 4 models per step and has drop rules, so it is less greedy in both respects.
- CKS grows from the simplest model towards more structure and stops by depth; our search starts from the most general model (one hole of all inputs) and specialises. A consequence: in CKS every intermediate model is a valid, more restrictive hypothesis that may fit badly; in ours every intermediate model can fit well, and the score has to reward specialisation, which the evidence does only weakly when the general GP already fits. Zhu22's remark on the global optimum is the same observation.
- ABCD's observation on constants (anti-correlated components, inflated intervals) and OAK's orthogonality constraint are two treatments of one problem; it applies to our sums of holes and to a hole next to a constant or a symbolic term.

### Gaps
- No timing, no count of marginal-likelihood evaluations, and no ablation of the number of restarts or of beam width in CKS or ABCD.
- Neither paper analyses how often the greedy path misses a better kernel; Malkomes et al. 2016 (Bayesian optimisation over kernels) criticise the greedy search (from memory, not checked).
- The source code repositories named in the papers (gp-structure-search, gpss-research) were not inspected for defaults beyond those stated in the text.

## 5. What techniques could we adopt, and what would each buy?

### Takeaway
The most direct gains are (i) the closed-form add/delete test for a linear term in the presence of a GP (Zhu22, Sec. 5), which would let the search screen a whole library per hole from one factorisation; (ii) orthogonal (constrained) kernels with analytic Sobol indices (OAK) to make sums of holes identifiable and to rank which splits to try; (iii) the closed-form active-subspace matrix (Wycoff21) as a noise-aware replacement for the "directions the hole does not depend on" part of the symmetry rule; (iv) weighting derivative-based regressions by the GP's posterior covariance of the derivatives (GP-SINDy), or putting the operator into the kernel (Raissi17), for the differential-equation experiment.

### Cited Findings
- Add/delete in closed form. With the current covariance C (noise + holes + included linear terms), a candidate column φ_i enters the model iff `q_i² > s_i`, with `q_i = φ_iᵀ C₋ᵢ⁻¹ (y−m)`, `s_i = φ_iᵀ C₋ᵢ⁻¹ φ_i`, and the evidence-optimal prior precision is `α_i = s_i²/(q_i² − s_i)`. The evidence splits as `L(α) = L(α₋ᵢ) + l(α_i)` with `l(α_i) = ½[log α_i − log(α_i + s_i) + q_i²/(α_i + s_i)]`. — [Zhu22, Sec. 5, Eqs. (25)–(36)](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- Initialise from "no GP" and let the GP grow. Zhu22 starts EM with the GP switched off to reach the optimum in which the equation dominates. — [Zhu22, Secs. 4 and 6](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- Restrict the inputs of the residual GP. Zhu22 gives the GP only the variable the missing physics is believed to depend on, and warns about identifiability otherwise. — [Zhu22, Sec. 7.2](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- Constrained kernels. `k̃ = k − E[S f(·)] E[S²]⁻¹ E[S f(·)]` makes each component integrate to zero under the input measure; an empirical-measure version works for any base kernel (Eq. 32). OAK reports a unique optimum over seeds where the unconstrained additive GP had three, and smaller component uncertainties. — [OAK, Sec. 3.1 Eq. (8), Sec. 3.4, App. E Eq. (32)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.09861)
- Sobol ranking of components from one fit: Eqs. (13)–(14), then truncation at a normalised index of 0.01. — [OAK, Sec. 4 and App. H](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.09861)
- Newton–Girard evaluation of all interaction orders in O(D²) per kernel entry. — [AGP, Sec. 3.3, Eq. (6)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1112.4394)
- Active-subspace matrix in closed form for Matérn-5/2 and Gaussian product kernels, with Monte Carlo intervals on its eigenvalues from hyperparameter uncertainty. — [Wycoff21, Sec. 3.1 Theorem 2, Sec. 3.3, App. A](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.11572)
- ARD length-scales are not a safe ranking of input relevance (the 1000-point counter-example). — [Wycoff21, Sec. 3.2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.11572)
- Operator in the kernel: if `f = L^φ u`, then φ is a kernel hyperparameter and is fitted by the exact joint evidence of observations of u and f. — [Raissi17, Sec. 2, Eqs. (2)–(4)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1701.02440)
- Derivatives with their uncertainty: posterior mean and variance of `∂f` from the kernel, and generalised least squares `Ĉ = (ΦᵀWΦ)⁻¹ΦᵀW U̇` with `W = Σ⁻¹`. — [GP-SINDy, Sec. 3.1.2 and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.11825)
- Selection by posterior inclusion probability instead of a coefficient threshold, and alternation between selecting terms and re-estimating the function under the selected equation. — [KBASS, Sec. 3 (end) and Sec. 4, Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05387v2)
- Search hygiene from kernel search: inherit parameters and restart only the new ones (10 restarts); penalise by BIC; distribute products over sums before counting parameters when interpretability matters; canonicalise by simplification rules; subtract constants from components. — [ABCD, Secs. 4, 6, 8, App. B.1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1402.4304)
- Component posteriors for display and for "look inside": `f₁ | f ~ N(μ₁ + K₁(K₁+K₂)⁻¹(f−μ₁−μ₂), K₁ − K₁(K₁+K₂)⁻¹K₁)`. — [CKS, Appendix](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.4922)
- Symbolic regression on the GP's predictor. EGP samples the fitted GP on a dense Sobol design (noise removed, as many points as wanted), runs an external symbolic regressor on it, splits the result into additive bases, removes dependent ones by SVD and keeps those whose coefficients are stable over bootstrap refits. — [EGP, Sec. 3, Fig. 4, Sec. 3.3](https://pmacslab.eng.uci.edu/files/2023/01/GP-Symbolic-Regression.pdf)
- Learned warp. A monotone warp `t + Σ a_i tanh(b_i(t + c_i))` fitted jointly with the GP by Eq. (6); the fitted warp "gives insight into the properties of the noise" and the shape of the transformation. — [WGP, Secs. 1, 3.1, 4](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2003/file/6b5754d737784b51ec5075c0dc437bf0-Paper.pdf)
- Staged hyperparameter estimation and caution on parameter meaning with a discrepancy term present. — [KOH, Secs. 4.3 and 4.5](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/statistics/comp_exp/jour.club/kennedy01.pdf)
- Designs that share coordinates (grids) allow exact checks of additivity; Latin hypercubes do not. — [arXiv 2610.10122, Abstract and Sec. 6 E3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.10122)
- Analytic propagation of a Gaussian input through a one-dimensional GP with SE kernel (mean and variance in closed form). — [GP-KAN, Sec. 3.1, Eq. (11)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18397)

### Inferences
What each would buy in our search (all of this is my assessment, not in the papers):
- Zhu22 add/delete test → the "peel" rule (`w·φ(u) + H(u)`), the "linear" rule's additive part, and "drop term" could be screened for every library function at fixed nonlinear parameters with one Cholesky factor of C and O(n²) per candidate, instead of a 20-iteration fit per candidate. At the optimum `α_i`, substituting into `l(α_i)` gives an evidence gain of `½[q_i²/s_i − 1 − log(q_i²/s_i)]` (my algebra from Eqs. 28–29), which can rank candidates before any refit. It applies only to terms that enter linearly with a Gaussian prior on the weight; library functions with inner parameters (`exp(c·v)`, `sin(a + b·u)`) need a grid or a local optimisation over those parameters around it.
- Zhu22 initialisation → an alternative to complexity penalties for steering towards the symbolic explanation; but it selects a local optimum by construction and does not give a score that is comparable across structures, so it fits a final "polish" step better than the beam search.
- OAK constrained kernels → (a) a sum `H(u₁) + H(u₂) + H(u₁,u₂)` becomes identifiable, so a single fit can tell how much the interaction hole carries, which is a direct test for the "no interaction" rule and a way to order the pairs to try; (b) a hole next to `w·b(x)` or a constant no longer trades mass with it, which should reduce the multiple optima that follow "peel" and "add hole". With uniform inputs in a box and Matérn-5/2 the integrals are one-dimensional and can be done once per length-scale by quadrature, or with the empirical measure (Eq. 32). For holes of symbolic inputs `u(x)` the input measure of u is not a product measure, so orthogonality between components holds only approximately; the empirical measure is the practical choice.
- OAK Sobol indices → a cheap "what to try first" ordering computed from the initial full GP: fit once with all orders up to 2 or 3, read which pairs carry interaction variance. This could replace trying "no interaction" on every pair. Evidence in the paper is for n of several hundred or more; at n ≈ 100 it would need checking on our benchmark.
- Wycoff21 → the "directions the hole does not depend on" and the "merge" rule (`v = u_j + c·u_k`; in log coordinates `v = u_j·u_k^p`) can be proposed from the eigenvectors of C computed in the hole's coordinates and in log coordinates. Compared with our present rule it uses the posterior covariance as well as the mean, averages over the box and not only the data points, and comes with intervals on the eigenvalues that can serve as the acceptance test. It finds linear combinations only, so it covers scaling and translation symmetries and unused directions, not power-law or exponential factors.
- Raissi17 → for the one-input differential-equation experiment, a candidate operator `L^φ = Σ_j p_j(u; φ) d^j/du^j` with polynomial coefficients is linear, so `L^φ H` is a GP with kernel `L L' k`. A homogeneous equation `L^φ H = 0` could be imposed as pseudo-observations of `L^φ H` equal to zero at collocation points, making φ a hyperparameter fitted by evidence and making candidate orders and degrees comparable by BIC. This extends the paper (which has data on both u and f); I have not seen it done in the papers read.
- GP-SINDy weighting → if the regression of derivative relations stays as it is, weighting the rows by the inverse posterior covariance of the derivative combinations is a small change that should stabilise the symmetry rule and the differential-equation fit near the box boundary, where derivative variance is largest (GP-SINDy notes the larger variance near boundaries, Sec. 4).
- KBASS → inclusion probabilities are an alternative to our fixed penalties per symbolic function; the EP machinery is heavy for our library sizes, and the Zhu22 ARD test gives a similar yes/no at lower cost.
- EGP → a "look inside" variant: run an external symbolic regressor on the noise-free posterior mean of a one- or two-input hole. It brings a second search engine in for exactly the sub-problems where ours stalls with a hole left, at the cost of a dependency; the bootstrap stability score is a cheap guard against spurious terms.
- WGP learned warp → fit a flexible monotone warp once on the initial GP and compare its shape with our five outer functions (exp, log, 1/m, √m), to decide which to try and in what order, instead of running all of them.
- KOH → a reminder for reporting: exponents and coefficients fitted next to a remaining hole are best-fitting values, not estimates of the true ones.
- arXiv 2610.10122 → if data can be chosen (the growth experiment), a design with repeated coordinates would give stronger support to additivity decisions than uniform random points.
- GP-KAN's propagation formula → of use only if we wanted exact uncertainty for a hole of a hole's output; not needed while all holes have symbolic inputs.
- From 2509.25946 I see nothing to adopt for a multi-input search; its symbolic-to-kernel initialisation is specific to one-dimensional series.

### Gaps
- None of these adoptions has been tested in our code; the benefits above are expectations.
- The orthogonal-kernel integrals for Matérn-5/2 under a uniform measure are stated to be analytic in OAK Sec. 3.2 but the formulas for that case are not in the sections I read.
- I did not check whether the Zhu22 add/delete identities remain numerically stable when the hole's kernel nearly spans the candidate column (the case `s_i → 0`).

## 6. How much does each overlap with our method?

### Takeaway
The building blocks are all in the literature (exact evidence of "linear terms + GP", greedy evidence-scored structure search, additive decompositions, warped likelihood, structure read from a fitted GP), but in separate papers. What I did not find in any of them: a GP placed inside a symbolic expression as `a(x)·H(u(x))`, rewrite rules that replace a GP by more specific symbolic structure, the evidence as the common score along that path, and symmetry detection from the fitted GP.

### Cited Findings
- Zhu22. Same: additive symbolic terms and an additive GP in one Gaussian model with exact evidence (Eqs. 11–14); the GP can remain in the answer; evidence-based inclusion of terms. Different: the library is fixed and enters linearly; no multiplicative factor on the GP, no symbolic inputs to the GP, no outer function; one GP whose inputs are chosen by the user; the preferred solution is reached by initialisation, not by comparing scores; the GP is "model discrepancy", not something to be resolved. — [Zhu22, Secs. 2–8](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/181580/1/YMSSP_108717_accepted%20version.pdf)
- KOH. Same: known model + GP for what it misses, analytic integration of linear coefficients. Different: the model form is given, hyperparameters are plugged in, and the purpose is calibration and prediction. — [KOH, Secs. 4.2–4.5](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/statistics/comp_exp/jour.club/kennedy01.pdf)
- CKS / ABCD. Same: greedy search by rewrite operators on an expression, each candidate a GP, scored by evidence at fitted parameters with a BIC penalty, parameters inherited from the parent, structure such as additivity, linear factors and periodicity found on the way, and the paper frames it as mirroring scientific discovery. Different: the expression is a kernel, not a function; base kernels act on single raw inputs; the search adds structure from a minimal start and ends by depth; the result is a kernel and a description in words, never a formula; beam width one. — [CKS, Secs. 2–3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.4922); [ABCD, Secs. 2–4, App. B](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1402.4304)
- AGP / OAK. Same: the hypothesis space of our "no interaction" rule (sums of GPs of fewer inputs). Different: all orders are in one kernel and weighted by continuous hyperparameters; selection is by hyperparameter fit and Sobol ranking after one fit, not by discrete rewrites; no symbolic content. OAK adds identifiability, which we do not have. — [AGP, Sec. 3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1112.4394); [OAK, Secs. 3–4](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.09861)
- Raissi17. Same: an unknown function with a GP prior, an equation's parameters estimated through the exact evidence. Different: the equation's form is given, it is a linear operator equation between two observed functions, and nothing is searched. — [Raissi17, Sec. 2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1701.02440)
- UDE and AI-Lorenz (gray-box). Same: known symbolic part plus a flexible function for the unknown part, later replaced by symbolic terms. Different: the flexible part is a neural network or basis expansion trained by a loss, with no evidence; replacement is a separate sparse or genetic regression on its output; the setting is differential equations and trajectories. — [UDE, Sec. 2.3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2001.04385); [AI-Lorenz, Sec. 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.14237)
- arXiv 2509.25946. Same: kernel-structure search with BIC in the score. Different: proposals and part of the score come from a vision-language model; one-dimensional series; symbolic regression is a separate mode or an initialiser. — [2509.25946, Secs. 3, 4.3, App. A.10](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25946)
- GP-KAN. Same: a function built from one-dimensional GPs composed through sums, close in form to `g(Σ H(u))`. Different: fixed architecture, inducing-point GPs trained as network weights, no evidence-based structure choice, no symbolic output, aimed at classification. — [GP-KAN, Secs. 3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18397)
- Wycoff21. Same: structure (a linear subspace of inputs) read from a fitted GP's derivatives. Different: closed form over the domain with posterior covariance; no further use in a model search; aimed at sequential design. — [Wycoff21, Sec. 3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.11572)
- KBASS, GPSINDy, GP-SINDy. Same: a kernel estimate of an unknown function and its derivatives, and linear relations among them selected sparsely, as in our symmetry rule and differential-equation experiment. Different: the relations are dynamics (`u_t = …`) over a dictionary; the kernel estimate is a pre-processor or a point estimate; no evidence of the combined model. — [KBASS, Secs. 3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05387v2); [GPSINDy, Sec. III](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.11076v3); [GP-SINDy, Sec. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.11825)
- WGP. Same: the Jacobian term for a function around the GP (Eq. 6). Different: the warp is a learned monotone function for the noise model, not a symbolic outer function chosen from a list. — [WGP, Sec. 3](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2003/file/6b5754d737784b51ec5075c0dc437bf0-Paper.pdf)
- EGP (found by search). Same: a GP model with symbolic terms found by search, the GP remaining for what the terms do not explain, an iteration that subtracts what is already found. Different: terms are additive bases in the mean only, found by genetic programming on GP-smoothed data with an MAE-complexity front, accepted by bootstrap stability of coefficients, parameters by MLE; no evidence comparison between structures; the aim is extrapolation of an emulator. — [EGP, Sec. 3](https://pmacslab.eng.uci.edu/files/2023/01/GP-Symbolic-Regression.pdf)
- SJEPA (found by search). Same on paper: symbolic law + GP correction integrated out, prior on symbolic structure, model comparison over a candidate set. Different: additive only, for latent dynamics, and not implemented in the reported experiments. — [SJEPA, Sec. 6.2, App. E.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04060)

### Inferences
- Ranking by closeness to our method: (1) Zhu22 for the model and score; (2) CKS/ABCD for the search; (3) EGP for the combination of symbolic search with a remaining GP; (4) OAK and Wycoff21 for reading structure from a fitted GP; (5) SJEPA Sec. 6.2 as a statement of intent. The differential-equation papers overlap only with our side experiment.
- Our model class can be written as a kernel-structure search with three kinds of kernel that CKS lacks: `a(x)a(x')·k(u(x), u(x'))` with symbolic a and u, rank-one kernels `σ_w² b(x)b(x')` for symbolic terms, and a warped likelihood. Seen this way the method is "compositional kernel search in which base kernels are parametrised by symbolic functions and the search prefers rank-one kernels", which is a fair one-line placement relative to the closest prior work.
- What is new relative to all papers read (within the limits of my search): (a) the hole as a first-class node of an expression, including multiplicative and nested-input positions; (b) the direction of search, from one general GP towards a formula, with the evidence comparable at every step; (c) rules that read symmetries and factors from the hole's posterior; (d) a final answer that may be part formula, part GP. Points (a) and (d) exist in additive form in Zhu22 and EGP.
- A point that Zhu22, SJEPA and ABCD each raise in their own terms and that applies to us: the evidence does not by itself single out the symbolic explanation when the GP can also fit; some extra preference (initialisation, priors, BIC, penalties) decides. Our penalties (BIC, 3 per symbolic function, 10 per non-simple exponent) play this role, and their values are a modelling choice of the same kind.

### Gaps
- I did not find, and so cannot rule out, work in the statistics literature on partially linear or semiparametric models that selects among nonlinear parametric forms by Bayes factors with a GP remainder; the queries used may not match its vocabulary.
- Whether the Bayesian machine scientist "hybrid" of Cohrs et al. 2024 integrates out its non-symbolic part is not stated on the page read.
- The comparison with AI Feynman's symmetry and separability tests, the closest non-GP analogue of our symmetry rule, belongs to another researcher's scope and was not read here.
